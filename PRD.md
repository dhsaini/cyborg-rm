## 1. Problem

A relationship manager covering ~100 wealth clients cannot answer, in the moment a
market event happens, the only question that matters: *which of my clients does this
actually affect, and what do I say to them?*

The information exists. It sits in a portfolio system that is organised by product,
not by exposure, and it cannot be queried in the shape the question is asked. So the
RM does one of two things — messages everyone with something generic, or messages
nobody. Both are failures. The first is noise the client learns to ignore; the second
is the silence a competitor fills.

The same gap exists in the other direction. When the product desk launches something
new, the RM has no systematic way to identify who it genuinely suits — so pitching
defaults to memory, recency, and whoever picks up the phone.

## 2. What this is

A relationship-manager copilot operating on a single RM's book, with two modes.

**Mode 1 — Reactive.** A market event or news headline is mapped to the subset of the
RM's clients whose portfolios carry real exposure to it, with a personalised outreach
draft per client.

**Mode 2 — Proactive.** A newly launched product *category* is matched against the same
book, producing a suitability-filtered pitch list **and** an explicit exclusion list
with stated reasons.

## 3. Scope boundary (structural, not cosmetic)

The tool operates on one RM's book. Never firm-wide.

This mirrors how wealth firms actually work: an RM has visibility into their own
clients' holdings and no one else's. The dataset carries `rm_id`; the interface
requires selecting an RM before anything renders.

This is a deliberate product decision, not a data limitation. A tool that surfaces
every client in the firm would be unusable in a real compliance environment.

## 4. Non-goals

- Not an advisory or recommendation engine. It drafts conversation openers, not advice.
- Not a portfolio analytics tool. No performance, attribution, or returns.
- Not a CRM. No client data is written, stored, or persisted.
- Not multi-RM. No aggregate desk or firm-level view.

## 5. Users

**Primary:** relationship manager at an Indian wealth management firm, 60–150 clients,
₹1Cr–₹200Cr AUM range.

**Actual audience for the demo:** WealthTech hiring managers and founders assessing
whether the author can turn a domain problem into a working AI product.

Where the two conflict, the demo audience wins — the tool must be legible in under a
minute to someone who has never seen it.

## 6. Data

100% synthetic. Generated by a seeded Python script (`numpy`/`pandas`, fixed seed),
never by an LLM — because the constraints below are correlated and regulatory, and an
LLM cannot hold them reliably across 1,000 rows.

### 6.1 Book structure — 1,000 clients across 10 RMs

Book sizes and client sizes both vary, as they do on a real desk.

| RM | Desk | Clients | Median AUM | Character |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| RM-01 | UHNI | 55 | ₹18Cr | PMS/AIF heavy, unlisted concentration |
| RM-02 | UHNI | 62 | ₹15Cr | promoter / business-owner skew |
| RM-03 | HNI senior | 88 | ₹6Cr | balanced, long tenure |
| RM-04 | HNI | 105 | ₹4Cr | mainstream |
| RM-05 | HNI | 112 | ₹3.5Cr | mainstream |
| RM-06 | HNI | 120 | ₹3Cr | mainstream |
| RM-07 | HNI | 98 | ₹4.5Cr | mainstream |
| RM-08 | Emerging | 145 | ₹1.8Cr | MF-dominant, minimal PMS, no AIF |
| RM-09 | Emerging | 135 | ₹2Cr | MF-dominant, SIP-heavier |
| RM-10 | Wealth | 80 | ₹5Cr | mainstream |

### 6.2 Hard constraints

These are regulatory minimums, not style choices. The generator must enforce them:

- **PMS:** `alloc_pms_pct × total_aum ≥ ₹50L`, else PMS = 0%
- **AIF:** `alloc_aif_pct × total_aum ≥ ₹1Cr`, else AIF = 0%

Consequence, and it is the intended one: AIF exposure becomes a UHNI marker
automatically. RM-08 and RM-09's books will contain almost no AIF — not because a rule
says so, but because the arithmetic forbids it. Constraints producing realism, rather
than realism being hand-authored.

### 6.3 Schema (29 fields)

| Group | Fields |
| --- | --- |
| Access | `client_id`, `rm_id` |
| Identity | `first_name`, `age`, `city_tier`, `segment`, `wealth_source`, `risk_profile`, `preferred_language` |
| Relationship | `relationship_tenure_years`, `days_since_last_contact` |
| Portfolio | `total_aum_inr`, `alloc_direct_equity_pct`, `alloc_equity_mf_pct`, `alloc_debt_mf_bonds_pct`, `alloc_pms_pct`, `alloc_aif_pct`, `alloc_unlisted_pct`, `alloc_gold_pct` |
| Exposure hooks | `top_sector_exposure`, `debt_duration_bucket`, `unlisted_holding_tag`, `intl_equity_exposure_pct` |
| Deployment behaviour | `sip_active`, `sip_monthly_inr`, `avg_lumpsum_ticket_inr`, `days_since_last_lumpsum`, `ledger_balance_inr` |
| Product-fit hook | `existing_thematic_exposure_pct` |

Notes:
- The eight `alloc_*_pct` fields sum to 100.
- `intl_equity_exposure_pct` and `existing_thematic_exposure_pct` are **sub-components**
  of existing equity allocations, not additional buckets. They do not enter the sum.
- HNI and UHNI both deploy predominantly in lumpsums, not SIPs. `avg_lumpsum_ticket_inr`,
  `days_since_last_lumpsum` and `ledger_balance_inr` are therefore stronger activity
  signals than SIP status, and are treated as such.

### 6.4 Seeding for demonstrability

Distributions are realistic, but the file is seeded so each frozen scenario (§7) hits
**8–15 clients within a given RM's book.** A tool that returns 60 of 100 clients is not
segmenting, it is spamming, and will be read that way.

## 7. Demo scenarios (frozen)

1. **RBI rate move** → `debt_duration_bucket`, `alloc_debt_mf_bonds_pct`
2. **IT sector selloff** → `top_sector_exposure`, `alloc_direct_equity_pct`
3. **Rupee depreciation / gold rally** → `intl_equity_exposure_pct`, `alloc_gold_pct`,
   and IT exposure as an *export beneficiary* — one headline, three client groups,
   three different reasons
4. **IPO filing by a company held unlisted** → `unlisted_holding_tag`

Scenario 3 is the clearest demonstration that segmentation is domain-driven rather
than keyword-driven.

## 8. Architecture

```
Streamlit UI
    → RM selection (mandatory gate)
    → Mode 1: headline  |  Mode 2: product category
    → Deterministic Python filter  ← all inclusion/exclusion decisions
    → Gemini API                   ← message drafting only
    → Drafts + reasons rendered
```

**Division of labour is the core design decision.** Deterministic Python decides *who*.
The LLM decides only *how to phrase it*.

Rationale: if a model decides suitability, the decision cannot be explained, reproduced,
or audited. "We cannot explain why this client was selected" is disqualifying in this
domain. Every inclusion and exclusion must trace to a rule that can be read in the source.

## 9. Compliance constraints

Non-negotiable, and enforced in code rather than in a disclaimer:

1. No named funds, AMCs, issuers, schemes, or NFOs. Products appear as anonymised
   categories, e.g. "AI & Technology Thematic Fund — Fund A".
2. No return projections, performance figures, or buy/sell/hold language in any
   generated output.
3. Drafts are framed as a reason to begin a conversation, never as a recommendation.
4. Mode 2 always renders the exclusion list alongside the pitch list, with reasons.
   This is structural. Exclusion *rules* are configuration and may change; the
   presence of the exclusion list may not.
5. In-app disclaimer visible on every screen.
6. No real client data, ever. No persistence of any kind.

## 10. UX requirement

The end-to-end path — select RM → input → filtered clients → draft — must be
completable in **four interactions or fewer**, and demonstrable in 60 seconds.

This is a hard constraint, not a preference: a 30–60 second walkthrough video is a
deliverable on the project page. A UI that cannot be shown in a minute is the wrong UI.

## 11. Definition of done

A public URL where, after selecting an RM:

- pasting any of the four frozen headlines returns the exposed subset of *that RM's*
  book, with a compliant draft per client;
- selecting a product category returns both a suitability-filtered pitch list and an
  exclusion list with stated reasons;
- the disclaimer is visible throughout;
- the whole path is demonstrable in under 60 seconds.

## 12. Out of scope (v1)

Authentication · persistence · CRM write-back · real market data feeds · WhatsApp API
integration (drafts are copy-to-clipboard) · multi-language generation (`preferred_language`
is a field, not a feature) · portfolio analytics · desk or firm-level aggregation.
